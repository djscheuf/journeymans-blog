Have you read 'The Effective Executive' by Drucker? First published in 1967, I still reference it regularly. One idea I keep coming back to is that knowledge workers are responsible for making their output effective. That means they have to know who will consume their output. This one fact alone has stark implications for our use of AI.

Consider this example. You're at the doctor to discuss some test results. Your doctor tells you "your Blah is 142." ... That info is useless to me. Do I need more exercise? Am I going to die? Is this elevated because of my height and genetics? The doctor is the only one who can make that translation. As a laymen, I lack the context to make that information useful. Further, it's simple lunacy to expect laymen to develop specialist-level understanding. After all, if I had to do so for every domain I interact with, I'd need multiple college degrees!

This goes way deeper than speaking plain English. Translation is about making your insight, your knowledge, useful to the audience. It's about translating the implications, the value judgments, into terms they care about. An altimeter reading going down could be good if you're landing the plane, or disaster if you weren't. The number means nothing without context about what matters to the person receiving it.

Now, let's turn to our use of AI. The AI wasn't in that meeting with the product owner. It doesn't know what was discussed, how it was said, or what "it" even refers to. When you use AI to generate code, or analyze data, all it is doing is following instructions. You are still the specialist responsible for translation! You, the human, must do work upfront to define what a "good" outcome means for your audience. You need guardrails on what goes into the AI process and validation on what comes out. You need to think about what will make the output useful before you touch the tool.

This isn't new. We've known this since the 60s. But AI forces you to answer "what is good?" before you start spending money to answer the question poorly. The specialist's responsibility hasn't changed. It's just become more painful if you can skip the translation work. You cannot skip the translation and expect useful, consistent results.

Are you defining what "good" looks like before you fire off that AI request? Or are you hoping the AI will figure out what your audience needs?
