Card 1 · The Talk in One Card

HR 658.5Card 1

The AI wave is early, but it is as big as electrification, mass manufacturing, and personal computing. Every one of those waves rewarded the same move: redesign the work, don’t just bolt the new machine onto the old process. Three lessons from those waves — and one warning — show you what to do about it.

History teaches us

Three lessons, one warning

Each lesson is filed under the era that taught it. The stock darkens as you go back in the catalog.

1

1850s · Industrial Revolution

Redesign your workflows

The way humans do the work will not be the best way for machines to do it. A sewing machine doesn’t sew like a human — same outcome, different method.

See also: Toyota’s looms stopped themselves rather than weave bad cloth faster.

2

1913 · Ford

To do more, start by doing less

How do you eat an elephant? One bite at a time. Small, single tasks that compose — then carry the work to the worker.

See also: The assembly line worked because each step was simple and the work flowed.

3

1990s · PC & Internet

Useful and accessible

To be used, a technology must be more valuable than it is inconvenient — and usable by the people who benefit, not just the librarians.

See also: Amazon beat Barnes & Noble because you could get books from home. Build tools, not just answers.

Bonus

Case study · Nestlé’s ERP

Who holds the bag?

When the workflow changes, the process shifts but the roles often don’t. The AI rollout will be the same management change.

Col. John Boyd: “People, Process, Technology — in that order!”

Card 2 · Do This Week

HR 658.5Card 2

Therefore

Put it to work on one workflow this week

Pick one workflow you own. Then:

1

Start with the end in mind

Ask: How would I do this today if I had none of my existing process or tools? What is actually important for this outcome? What would it take for one person to do all of it?
2

Compose simple tasks into complex jobs

Break the outcome into small tasks, then sort each: Automatable (routine, repeated, consistent), Augmentable (numerous, varied, but clear), or Must remain human (nuanced or vague judgment calls).

3

Define done, so you can check the work

If you can’t say what “done” looks like, you can’t verify what the machine produced — and you hold the bag.
